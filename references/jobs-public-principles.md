# Design Principles from Steve Jobs’s Public Record

This reference turns verifiable public material into product decisions. It is not a biography, and it does not treat Steve Jobs as the sole author of Apple’s design. Each principle separates source evidence, a transferable inference, and its limit.

## 1. Design the complete object, not the surface

**Source:** [Steve Jobs Archive: Make Something Wonderful](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), including the 1983 Aspen design talk and 1984 interviews; see also [Objects of Our Life](https://stevejobsarchive.com/stories/objects-of-our-life).

**Evidence:** Jobs argued that computers would enter workplaces, schools, and homes, so industrial design, software design, and the way people interact with products deserved serious attention together. “Looking good” and “working well” are not separate design problems.

**Transferable rule:** Treat naming, form, interaction, content, packaging, sound, service, and maintenance as one product decision. Before shipping software, inspect first launch, core tasks, recovery, and updates. Before approving hardware, inspect grip, ports, charging, storage, cooling, packaging, and support.

**Limit:** This does not require every product to be expensive, closed, or difficult to repair. It requires every touchpoint to serve the product promise.

## 2. Excellence comes from judgment and persistence

**Source:** [Steve Jobs Archive: Make Something Wonderful](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), including the 1984 interview with Michael Moritz.

**Evidence:** Jobs described studying Cuisinarts, cars, wine labels, paintings, and other everyday objects. He connected taste with observation, mistakes, refinement, and the stubbornness to keep improving a product.

**Transferable rule:** Aesthetic quality is not a one-time flash of inspiration. Build a reference library, make comparable directions, prototype quickly, and make the team name where an option is merely functional versus complete. Spend time on high-frequency touchpoints and details people can actually perceive.

**Limit:** Persistence does not replace engineering validation, user evidence, regulation, safety, or budget discipline. “One more refinement” must create perceptible value.

## 3. Simplicity absorbs complexity

**Sources:** [Apple HIG: Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles/) and [The Guts of a New Machine](https://designobserver.com/the-guts-of-a-new-machine/), originally published in *The New York Times Magazine* in 2003.

**Evidence:** Simplicity comes from focus, subtraction, and clear hierarchy. Difference is a consequence of a better solution, not the goal by itself.

**Transferable rule:** Define the essential task, remove repetition and ornamental noise, and place complexity in defaults, automation, system design, and reliable recovery. Do not create the appearance of simplicity by hiding capability, shrinking text, or creating empty space without purpose.

## 4. Focus means repeatedly saying no

**Source:** [1997 WWDC Closing Chat transcript](https://allaboutstevejobs.com/videos/keynotes/wwdc_1997_closing_chat). This is a public transcript, not current Apple documentation.

**Evidence:** Jobs described focus as saying no to many reasonable things so a small number of products could become a coherent whole.

**Transferable rule:** Maintain an explicit “do not build” list. Require every new feature, material, interaction, and structure to name the product promise it serves. Defer or remove choices that only advertise capability, follow a competitor, or increase cognitive load.

**Limit:** Saying no is not a license for unilateral decisions. Safety, accessibility, legal, privacy, and critical user needs require evidence and accountability. Record why something was rejected and when it should be reconsidered.

## 5. Humanities and technology should meet in the product

**Sources:** [Smithsonian Oral and Video Histories: Steve Jobs](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/comphist/sj1.html), an interview from April 20, 1995, and the [Smithsonian archive record](https://www.si.edu/object/archives/components/sova-nmah-ac-1303-ref666).

**Evidence:** Jobs rejected a simple separation between art, science, and engineering. He saw excellent technology as a form of expression that should make complex capability understandable and useful to ordinary people.

**Transferable rule:** Let engineering constraints shape form, and let design goals influence architecture. Translate technical capability into clear actions rather than exposing technical vocabulary. Type, latency, error handling, and sound are product quality just as structure, tactility, and cooling are.

## 6. Details are relationships, not isolated decoration

**Source:** [Design Observer’s reprint of Rob Walker’s article](https://designobserver.com/the-guts-of-a-new-machine/), originally published in *The New York Times Magazine* in 2003.

**Evidence:** Simplicity becomes powerful when purpose, content, operation, structure, and manufacturing are considered together. Surface styling cannot repair a broken product relationship.

**Transferable rule:** Review state changes, first actions, waiting, failure, recovery, edges, seams, sound, and long-term use instead of reviewing only a screenshot or render. Details should form one grammar rather than each being independently “polished.”

## 7. Cultural input becomes product capability later

**Sources:** [Stanford: You’ve got to find what you love](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says), the 2005 commencement address, and the [Stanford video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UF8uR6Z6KLc).

**Evidence:** Jobs connected a calligraphy class, typefaces, letter spacing, and typography to later Macintosh design. The lesson is not that every designer should study calligraphy; it is that cross-disciplinary input can change future product judgment.

**Transferable rule:** Build references across architecture, furniture, publishing, photography, sound, mechanics, materials, film, and behavior. Convert references into explainable product decisions instead of collecting images or copying styles.

## 8. User insight and active invention belong together

**Sources:** The [BusinessWeek 1998 interview archive](https://gadgetsnow.indiatimes.com/Interview-with-BusinessWeek-1998/amp_photolist/10257580.cms) and the [WGBH 1990 interview transcript](https://product-think.blogspot.com/2024/02/interview-with-steve-jobs-wgbh-1990.html).

**Evidence:** Jobs distinguished incremental research from discovering an experience that does not yet exist. This is often reduced to “people don’t know what they want until you show them.”

**Transferable rule:** Use observation, interviews, data, and usability tests for known problems. Use prototypes, demonstrations, trials, and real contexts for new paradigms. Never use this principle to skip research or turn a team’s preference into a prediction of the future.

## 9. The combined operating questions

| Review question | Desired answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why does the product exist? | One sentence covering user value and emotional value. |
| What should it be excellent at? | One clear priority task or experience. |
| What did you remove? | A reasoned tradeoff with impact and a test plan. |
| Who carries the complexity? | The system, defaults, service, and craft carry more of it than the user. |
| Is software and hardware one product? | Actions, feedback, materials, content, and service share semantics. |
| What appears up close? | Purposeful details, honest materials, and complete boundary states. |
| What happens after failure? | Clear explanation, recovery, control, and preserved trust. |
| Does it work for more people? | Accessibility, culture, localization, input, and context are considered. |
| Where is the evidence? | Prototypes, real contexts, usability, accessibility, durability, or manufacturing validation. |

## 10. Quote and attribution discipline

- “Design is not just what it looks like and feels like. Design is how it works.” is commonly attributed to a 2003 Jobs interview. Link to the [reprinted article](https://designobserver.com/the-guts-of-a-new-machine/) and do not use the sentence as a context-free slogan.
- “People don’t know what they want until you show it to them” has several retellings and secondary versions. Use it only as a warning against relying on surveys alone when exploring a new paradigm.
- “Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication” is frequently mixed with Apple advertising and Jobs-related attribution. Treat it as an inspirational line; use current HIG guidance and evidence for actual rules.
- Jobs’s personal behavior, management style, and product principles are different things. This Skill extracts design-relevant public material without imitating his personality or power relationships.

## 11. How to state a conclusion

Do not say, “Jobs would definitely choose this.” Say:

> This choice better supports focus, a complete experience, and complexity absorbed by the product because … The current evidence is … The next validation is …

That keeps the judgment sharp without turning a historical figure into a substitute for evidence.
